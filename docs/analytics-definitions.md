# Analytics Definitions

These definitions constrain future analytics; no analytics are implemented during
Foundation. Analytics operate on versioned structured match data, not directly on
detector tensors or prose generated by an LLM.

## Evidence and reporting

Every metric should carry its unit, population/filter, contributing record IDs,
calculation version, and data-quality coverage where relevant. Unknown inputs yield
an unavailable or qualified result, not an invented value. LLMs may explain
computed statistics but must never create or silently repair them.

## Initial vocabulary

- **Rally:** the interval from a valid serve contact to a rally-ending condition,
  represented by explicit boundary evidence and confidence.
- **Player court position:** the homography projection of an estimated
  ground-contact point on the court plane, never a person-box center.
- **Distance traveled:** sum of quality-gated consecutive court-plane player
  displacements, in meters. Reports must name smoothing and missing-data policy.
- **Speed:** displacement divided by elapsed time, in meters per second, reported
  only where the time base and position coverage satisfy a declared quality gate.
- **Court occupancy:** time or frame share within a named, versioned court region.
- **Bounce location:** court-plane location associated with a bounce event; it is
  not the projection of an arbitrary airborne ball observation.
- **Shot:** an interval anchored by a paddle-contact event and ending at the next
  contact, bounce, or rally end according to a versioned rule set.
- **Hitter:** player identity associated with a contact event, retaining assignment
  confidence and allowing `unknown`.

## Missing and interpolated data

Observed, interpolated, and missing positions remain distinguishable throughout
aggregation. A metric must state whether bounded interpolation is accepted. Long
gaps cannot be bridged to inflate distance, speed, trajectory length, shot count,
or rally duration.

## Confidence

Do not multiply unrelated confidence values and present the result as calibrated.
Event and metric confidence must eventually be defined and evaluated for that
specific output. Until then, surface component confidences and coverage separately.
